# Spend modes

**Name the mode before ranking purchases.** The same candidate list ranks completely differently
under each, so ranking first and picking a stance afterwards produces incoherent advice. Pick the
mode from whichever growth term dominates the resource — the three unusual levers reward opposite
behaviour, so misreading which one dominates inverts the right policy.

**HOARD — when interest dominates.** Income proportional to stock means spending directly slows
compounding. In the observed extreme, a stock's interest income ran ~26 orders of magnitude above its
production rate: buying production was worthless and hoarding *was* growth. But bounded spending
against a compounding stock is quantifiably cheap — spending 1 % of stock costs about four seconds of
progress. Compute the cost; hoard mode is not a taboo.

**BURST — when a missing-percent window is open.** Those fills read *capacity*, not current stock, so
the emptier you are the bigger the fill. Spend everything as fast as you can and keep the tank empty
until the window closes. This is also the mode at cap: with income already overflowing, marginal
holding is worth nothing and an expensive purchase costs zero time. Food fruits are the confirmed
window-openers, they stack across overlapping types, and they are budgeted against Toxicity.

**REST — when a resting rate is charging.** Some stocks gain faster the longer they go untouched, and
the bonus dies the moment something transacts. A standing dribble of automatic activity suppresses
the lever permanently, so resting a resource is an active choice to route activity elsewhere, not
neglect. Waiting for a full Toxicity drain before an item burst is exactly this mode.

**REINVEST — when rate dominates.** Buy the printer. The second level of an early generator roughly
doubles current production, so generator levels outrank almost everything under scarcity. This is the
default mode; most of the time you are fine with just rate.

## Never generate into a cap

Production into a full sink is discarded outright. Check the sink before scheduling the generator,
and bench a generator whose resource is sitting at capacity — its marginal income is zero and its
spell spot has better uses.

Discrete-payout resources can sit above cap for a short grace period, after which a rubber band pulls
them back down, and any discrete touch restarts the grace. **Overcap stock is a decaying asset unless
something keeps touching it**, so treat it as spend-now value, not as savings.

## Never spend to exactly zero

Large-number rounding can zero the remainder instead of leaving a small value. Leave an epsilon.

## The three reasons to hold

Hold-versus-spend is a computation, never a habit. There are exactly three reasons to hold:

1. **Goal reservation** — it is the frontier currency of the active milestone.
2. **Interest** — income scales with stock, so spending slows compounding.
3. **Stock-as-modifier** — the holding itself grants an effect you are using, as holding Thaumaturgy
   raises mana capacity and can bridge a capacity gap without a detour lap.

If none of the three applies, holding is delay with extra steps.
